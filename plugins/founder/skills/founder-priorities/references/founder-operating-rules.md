# Founder Operating Rules

Use this reference when auditing founder time, focus, and recurring execution traps.

## Operating Principles

- The founder's calendar should reflect the real bottleneck.
- Protect decision time and deep work.
- Choose fewer priorities and finish them.
- Use simple operating cadences that survive bad weeks.
- State unknowns instead of fabricating motives or opportunity costs.

## Required Evidence

Before prescribing a new operating plan, gather:

- stage and current founder job
- bottleneck hypothesis
- representative calendar or time sample
- active commitments and hard constraints
- outcome evidence for major recurring activities

If evidence is sparse, ask for it or label estimates explicitly.

## Common Traps

- Letting inbox and chat define priorities
- Mistaking activity for progress
- Carrying work that should be delegated or killed
- Copying another company's playbook without stage fit
- Optimizing side quests while deferring the hard conversation
- Treating every meeting as waste regardless of stage-specific purpose

## Calendar Audit

Classify one representative week by purpose and intended outcome, not by meeting format:

| Purpose | Examples of intended outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Customer signal | discovery, sales, support that informs product or revenue |
| Product / delivery | building, shipping, quality review |
| People / recruiting | hiring loops, coaching tied to a clear capacity need |
| Coordination | decisions, unblocking, escalation |
| Admin / intake | inbox, scheduling, compliance chores |
| Deep work | uninterrupted creation or analysis |
| Learning / reflection | deliberate research or synthesis with a decision attached |

Customer discovery, sales, recruiting, and product reviews may be the founder's stage-specific job. Do not auto-classify them as low-value "management."

Then:

1. Measure the actual mix against the current bottleneck.
2. Batch low-signal coordination and admin into fewer windows.
3. Protect deep work and decision blocks before accepting new meetings.
4. Keep stage-critical customer, sales, recruiting, or product time when it is the job.
5. Review after two weeks from observed output, not generic percentage targets.

## Focus By Evidence

For each major activity, estimate the time, cash, or attention it consumes and the result it produces. Rank by output-to-input ratio, then choose what to double down on, improve, delegate, or stop. Treat 80/20 as a search heuristic, not a promised distribution. Label estimates when measurement is incomplete.

## Challenge Protocol

1. State the real decision and the current inclination.
2. Name the assumption doing the most work.
3. Identify a possible avoidance pattern as a testable hypothesis, and quantify the cost of another delay only when evidence supports the estimate.
4. Compare the current approach with stage-appropriate alternatives and the observable evidence an operator would inspect.
5. Recommend one concrete action and the evidence that would invalidate it.

Challenge the decision, not the person. Directness does not justify fabricated motives, false certainty, or a hostile tone.

## Founder Proximity

When quality, culture, or customer understanding is deteriorating, check whether the founder is too far from the work. Useful interventions include direct customer conversations, reviewing the actual product or service, and skip-level listening to restore signal.

Do not recommend removing a management layer as a generic intervention. Define the bottleneck, the proximity action, and an exit condition. Do not turn founder involvement into blanket micromanagement.

## Cutting Work Safely

When capacity is exceeded:

1. Stop future irreversible commitments first.
2. Cut or pause low-signal work in descending order of reversibility.
3. Escalate people, contractual, or legal commitments for explicit human judgment.
4. Prefer deferral or delegation over destructive resets when information is incomplete.

## Stage-Fit Traps And Recovery

Common early-stage traps include building before validating demand, underpricing without unit economics, hiring before the work is repeatable, spreading across too many segments or channels, and polishing brand or infrastructure before the offer works.

Recovery should start with current evidence:

- identify the segment, customer, product, or channel already producing the strongest signal
- stop future irreversible waste and cut low-signal work by reversibility
- run the smallest test that could change the decision

## Boundaries

- Runway, burn, pricing, and hiring ROI belong to financial analysis guidance.
- Launch go / narrow / delay decisions belong to launch readiness guidance.
- Personnel restructuring, contract exits, and legal commitments require human judgment and qualified counsel where applicable.
