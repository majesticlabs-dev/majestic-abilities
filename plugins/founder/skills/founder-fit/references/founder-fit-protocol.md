# Founder Fit Protocol

Supporting material for the founder-fit skill. Use the prompt bank for the self-portrait interview, the presses for evidence quality, the five tests for the distillation gauntlet, and the templates for file structure.

## Self-portrait prompt bank

Offer one prompt per exchange. An answer counts when it meets the prompt's spirit, even when it misses the frame.

### Drive prompts

- Even as a kid, I would ..., and I am still drawn to it.
- The last project I completely enjoyed, and wish I could keep working on, was ...
- Even on vacation, I cannot help it; I still ...
- My friends laugh when I start talking about ... because I get so excited I cannot stop.
- Whenever I ..., I get lost in the work and feel energized when it ends.
- If I could go back to school, I would study ...
- I was surprised how much my peers praised my work when I ...
- I love doing ... and I cannot justify it, but there it is.
- My self of ten years ago would be surprised that lately I love it whenever ...
- If I could go back in time, I would tell myself to worry less about ... and more about ...

### Anti-questions (hindrances)

- When I am faced with ..., I feel it in the pit of my stomach.
- The last time I had to ..., I did an embarrassingly minimal job because I could not bring myself to do better.
- I have an intense dread of any meeting where we ...
- If my job required ... with even 10 percent of my time, I would think about changing jobs.
- When I am faced with ..., I procrastinate so desperately I do chores I normally avoid.
- I am embarrassed about ..., I do not want to work on it, and I would genuinely be happier avoiding it forever.
- Honestly, though I would love to be great at ..., I am never going to be good at it.
- Whenever I ... all day, the work is good but I am wrecked for the evening.
- I know I am supposed to like ..., but I never get excited about it.

### Outside-in homework

Give these three questions, adapted to second person, to two to five people who know the person well. Mixed contexts beat five colleagues.

1. Construct the absolutely perfect set of circumstances, in subject matter, incentives, goal, team, and direction, where this person absolutely kills it: efficient, productive, happy, and making everyone around them happy.
2. What is this person's personal hell? Construct the scenario where they die inside, mess everything up, and upset everyone.
3. What are this person's strengths that they are unaware of because the strengths come so naturally?

Question 3 answers are the crown jewels: whatever comes both naturally and powerfully is the edge, and it is definitionally invisible to the person.

## The presses

- **Label without episode:** state the last time it happened, what was done, and who said what. No episode after honest digging means the trait is not recorded.
- **Sanitized motive:** ask for the version that would only be admitted privately. Money, fame, and proving a point are legitimate motives.
- **Aspiration dressed as fact:** ask what the record of the last five years says.
- **Generic phrasing:** press "I like building things" into building what, for whom, and when.
- **Energized-or-wrecked check:** after a full day of the claimed skill, buzzing or dead? Skilled-but-draining is recorded as exactly that compound fact.

## The five voter tests

Every voter candidate passes all five. A candidate that passes some tests hard but misses one decisive axis is a special strength, not a voter.

1. **Extremity, earned.** Evidence is a history: years of obsession, care beyond what the job required, depth nobody asked for. Acquired last quarter or aspired to next year is not a voter.
2. **Rarity among peers.** A well-informed peer asked to name the person or company for this trait would name you. A top-1 percent trait that several peers also hold is a shaping strength. When reputation is the claim, ask for the citable artifact.
3. **Decisive.** Name the real trade-offs customers who value the trait happily accept: the weak companion product, the refused segments, the premium price. No named cost means the voter is unproven.
4. **Reverberation.** The trait forces second-order decisions in product, pricing, market, hiring, and what is refused to ship. If naming it changes nothing downstream, it is a slogan.
5. **Energizes.** The trait is executed for years at an intensity nobody sustains through discipline alone. For a company, the test applies to the people who actually carry the obsession.

Voter tiers:

- **Voter (V1, V2):** passes all five. At most two.
- **Special strength:** genuinely top-1 percent and passing some tests hard, but missing one decisive axis, usually rarity or an unnamed overpowered cost. A secondary pillar the strategy should deploy, never inflated into a voter.
- **Shaping strength:** true and strategy-relevant but common. Recorded, not decisive.
- **Cut:** aspiration, virtue word, or no evidence.

## File templates

### Self-portrait

```markdown
# Who, me - <name>

> In progress. Prompts covered, current candidate, parked candidates,
> homework status. If you are resuming, continue here. (Removed at
> finalization.)

What decision this portrait serves, if named. Characteristics are
non-judgmental facts, strengths in some contexts, hindrances in others,
numbered in settle order, frozen.

## The shape

One or two paragraphs reading the whole portrait back, cited by
characteristic numbers, answering the framing question if one was named.

## Drives

**M1.** <The fact, specific and unsanitized.>
Evidence: <episodes, dated and concrete>.
Expresses as strength when: <contexts>.
Expresses as hindrance when: <contexts>.

## Hindrances

**M2.** <The dread or drain fact, stated plainly, not a to-fix item.>
Evidence: <episodes>.
Cost if ignored: <where this quietly ruins things>.
Route around it by: <context choices, not self-improvement vows>.

## The edge (outside-in)

**M3.** <What comes so naturally the person could not see it, attributed.>

## Tensions

- M1 vs M2: <the contradiction and what survives>.

## Outside-in homework

Asked <who, context> on <date>. The three questions, adapted for sending.

## Next steps

Distill this file to the one or two decisive traits a strategy can be
built on.
```

### Voters

```markdown
# Voters - <person or company>

> In progress. Candidate list with sources, episode notes, and status.
> Currently at the gauntlet with tests run so far. If you are resuming,
> continue here. (Removed at finalization.)

Whose voters, which input files this distills, what strategy question
it serves.

## The voters

### V1 - <short name, three to seven words>

**<One vivid sentence stating the voter.>**

- **Extremity, earned** - <the years, episodes, unreasonable investments>.
- **Rarity** - <why a well-informed peer names them; the citable artifact>.
- **Overpowers** - <the trade-offs its fans happily accept>.
- **Reverberates into** - <the second-order decisions it forces>.
- **Energizes** - <who carries the obsession and the evidence it runs on joy>.

## Special strengths

- <Trait> - <which tests it nails, the one it misses, why the strategy
  should still deploy it>. [<source numbers>]

## Shaping strengths

- <Trait> - <one line: real, but common>. [<source numbers>]

## The verdict (only when zero voters survive)

<Nothing is special and extreme yet, plus the twofold response with
named obsession candidates.>

## Cut at the gauntlet

- <Candidate> - <one line of reasoning>.

## Next steps

Audit strategy against the voters across pricing, positioning, product,
and channels. Mismatches are where to pivot.
```

Identifiers freeze once written. Wording may sharpen under a frozen identifier while the file is in progress.
