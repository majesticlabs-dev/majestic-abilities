# Headline Evidence Base

Use this evidence as calibration, not as a formula for guaranteed performance. Most large-scale results come from one outlet and one era. Audience, channel, trust, and the truth of the underlying story remain more important than any word-level prior.

## Dataset substrate

The Upworthy Research Archive contains 32,487 randomized experiments, 150,817 content packages, and 538 million assignments from 2013 to 2015.

- Matias et al., *Scientific Data* (2021): <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8329003/>
- A 2024 correction found unreliable randomization in about 22 percent of tests.
- Packages included images recorded only as IDs, so an apparent headline effect can partly reflect image choice.

Do not present this archive as a universal sample of news, B2B, product, or email behavior.

## Empirical priors

### Specificity, people, and numbers

Banerjee and Urminsky found that numbers, concreteness, and stories about individuals often increased clicks in the Upworthy corpus. Direct reader address slightly decreased clicks there, so "you" is not a universal advantage.

Source: <https://home.uchicago.edu/ourminsky/Banerjee_Urminsky_Headlines.pdf>

Apply this by using a number only when it is verified and meaningful in context. Precision without context can mislead.

### Emotional language

Robertson et al. reported an association of roughly 2.3 percent higher click-through rate for each additional negative word in tested news headlines. Gligoric et al. reported a preregistered replication. This does not justify fear, distortion, or sensational wording.

- <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01538-4>
- <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0289578>

Use real stakes when the evidence supports them. Never add negativity merely to chase clicks.

### Informative length

Aubin Le Quere and Matias found an inverted-U relationship for concreteness: adding information helped vague headlines but hurt already-concrete ones. Both blind teases and exhaustive summaries can underperform.

Source: <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9>

Preserve the subject and core fact while leaving open only a question the story genuinely answers.

### Prediction ceiling

Word-level features predicted the winning member of an A/B pair only about 55 percent of the time in one large analysis. This weak ceiling supports generating materially different candidates and testing them when possible instead of claiming one formula will win.

Source: <https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~ashton/pubs/text-success-upworthy-plosone2021.pdf>

## Channel caveats

- Web-headline length findings do not override mechanical subject-line truncation.
- Editorial click-through results do not automatically transfer to press releases, ads, or B2B pitches.
- A subject line cannot repair a pitch sent to the wrong audience.
- A high-performing headline can still be inaccurate, unethical, or damaging to trust.
- Historical exemplars teach compression and framing, not guaranteed performance.

## Application rule

Treat these findings as prompts for candidate diversity. Final selection must prioritize factual support, audience relevance, channel fit, and earned trust.
